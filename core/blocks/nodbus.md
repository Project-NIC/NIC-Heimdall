# NodBus — the protocol and its clock

> **Design-stage concept — nothing built.** The figures are verified against the parts' sheets and
> the parts before a board is made.

NodBus is the 40 B TDMA bus behind a Bifrost and, one width down, the 16 B NodBus mini behind an
Argus. This document owns the network clock, the link the protocol sits on, how a bus starts and
heals, the rung, the slot schedule, the one-second path and the delay tables. **It wins on any bus
question.** The frames themselves are `../PROTOCOL.md`'s.

## The network clock — the one place it is defined

**Every other document references this section and repeats no numbers of its own; where another
document differs, this section wins.**

- **Timebase: 2²³ Hz = 8 388 608 Hz.** All time arithmetic is in 2²³ ticks: one tick ≈ 119 ns;
  128 frames/s × 2¹⁶ ticks a frame = 2²³ exactly; frame rates are powers of two (the `BUSCFG`
  codes); a 23-bit shift of a tick count is seconds. Everything divides exactly — shifts, never
  division.
- **Born on Kronos**, a GNSS-disciplined PLL, and **distributed on the M-LVDS time bus** — one
  `DS91C176` driver per pair on Kronos, a `THVD1450` receiver on every tap, one ribbon along the
  card row. The Mayak takes both pairs; its firmware uses PPS-K and the I²C label and leaves the
  clock alone. **The clock enters every node on the HSE bypass input**: on a unit the data body's
  `CLK` is wired straight to `OSC_IN`, and the switch from the RC boot onto the wire is software in
  the clock tree — no gate, no pin remap. **A card is the one exception**: its two sources, the
  time bus and the up port, each pass one 3-state buffer into `OSC_IN` — a `74AUP1G126` and a
  `74AUP1G125`, both enabled from `ATTN` with opposite polarity, so exactly one drives and the
  other source is not looked at.
- **On every wire: 2²² = 4 194 304 Hz. 2²³ exists only on a board.** Kronos's time bus and every
  spur, inside the box or out of it, carry 2²², and no wire carries more. The number is the floor
  of the processors' own HSE — the 4 MHz class on both the H523 and the H7A3 — and two media put
  the clock there:
  - **Copper**: halved emissions and halved sensitivity to cable capacitance. The isolated
    transceiver does not bind (the `ISO145x` runs to 50 Mb/s); 500 m of cable behind it does.
  - **Glass**, the harder wall: the optical family is **DC-coupled with idle mapped to
    laser-dark** (`../../galvani/HARDWARE.md`), and the fastest DC-coupled catalogue part is the
    **10 Mb/s** module. A 2²² square wave is 8,39 M transitions a second and fits it; 2²³ would
    want ~16,8 Mb/s, where no DC-coupled part exists. **That module is on the station for the
    clock channel and no other.**
- **Reception on a node:** 2²² on HSE → **÷2 first**, from one edge polarity, so the cable's duty
  distortion and edge jitter die before the multiplier → a clean 2²¹ reference → **×4 = 2²³
  on-board**. An integer PLL's loop filter smooths jitter, but it multiplies whatever edges it is
  given; only the ÷2 off one polarity is immune to duty distortion, so the ÷2 kills the duty and
  the PLL polishes the rest. A fractional PLL is out — it adds spurs of its own. Where the ÷2→×4
  path does not fall out of a part's clock tree, any integer HSE→PLL synthesis to 2²³ does the
  same job; **what is decided is the wire frequency.** **The PLL input window is 2–16 MHz on both
  parts** (H523 DS14540 Rev 3 Table 46, H7A3 DS13195 Table 55): 2²² sits in it, and 2²¹ — a node's
  reference after its ÷2 — clears the 2 MHz floor by 4,9 %.
- **Kronos hands the cards 2²²** — its PLL's Q output, 2²⁸ ÷ 64. **A Bifrost passes it to its
  ports undivided**: the clock that arrives on the ribbon leaves on the card's `MCO1` at prescaler
  1, through the port buffer, so every spur carries Kronos's own edge. An Argus takes 2²² off its
  up port and puts `MCO1` at ÷8. Both roles lock one PLL to 2²⁷ — `M` 1 · `N` 64 — so the card has
  one clock tree, and `ATTN` picks the source and the prescaler.
- **NodBus mini runs its own rung: 2¹⁹ = 524 288 Hz, one number, every segment, generated by
  Argus.** The rung is the **card's role, not the segment's** — a Bifrost hands its four ports
  2²², an Argus its four segments 2¹⁹, out of `MCO1` through one `SN74LVC126A` quad buffer, each
  gate's `OE` on a GPIO of its own — and the `ATTN` level that names the role names the rung with
  it. No mini-NOD wants more, and the 2 Mb/s optical module a long segment may carry could not
  give more. A communication board's `ID` resistor still says what its medium carries; the card
  does not read it to pick a clock. **The mini data rung is the same pair as everywhere: 2²⁰
  alone, 2²¹ chained.**
- **At the mini-NOD the clock lands on a timer's external clock input**, not on HSE: the sample
  grid is an integer division of the sync — at 2¹⁹, ÷8192 = 64 Hz, ÷4096 = 128 Hz, ÷2048 = 256 Hz,
  all powers of two. **The unit's core is out of the time path**: between two sync edges lie
  1,907 µs, in which a ±1 % source moves 19 ns. The error is the edge, not the interval — capture,
  divider propagation and the two optical modules, all ns-class and fixed, so the card's ranging
  takes it out. **No mini unit carries a crystal: the rung disciplines the HSI through the PLL** —
  `TIM5` gates the core against the rung and the error goes into `FRACN`, one clock tree on Gauss,
  Pascal and `Quark-Tubes`; what it holds is the UART's baud and the ranging turnaround.
- **On a mini segment, losing the sync edges is losing the clock** — the unit mutes and rejoins
  when the edges come back (*The clock is the kill switch*, below). What a mini-NOD watches is the
  **edge stream**, not a frequency: a count of missing edges is the trigger.

## The link under the protocol

The link is the common Galvani base — channel A the data, channel B the clock, the two bodies,
the barrier, the ladder (`../../galvani/README.md`). NodBus adds channel B populated with the wire
clock, TDMA fired off it, the ranging pulse, and the 40 B / 16 B frame pair. ModBus rides the same
base with channel B unpopulated; a Galvani board never knows which protocol is speaking. **All of
the 485 is on the Galvani board**: no node, card or clock board carries a 485 link, and inside the
enclosure there is none — UART and I²C as wires, the clock and PPS to a unit as wires behind a
buffer, and Kronos's time bus received on `THVD1450`s used only as receivers of a pair that never
leaves the box. The cable is outdoor UTP Cat 6, glanded into the enclosure onto a board-mounted
socket, never an inline connector outside.

- **Every NodBus run is full duplex.** The feed travels in a 2-core cable of its own (2× 1,5 mm²
  or 2,5 mm², 300/500 V class), so the four pairs of every copper run are **data TX · clock ·
  signal ground · data RX**; glass never carries power. There is no half-duplex NodBus build. The
  split that matters downstream is **shared segment against point-to-point**, which is copper
  against glass. On glass the always-open return channel keeps the line delay measurable in
  operation and lets a critical frame go **twice** under interference at no protocol cost, the
  frame's own index making the duplicate idempotent; a copper run has the same two things.
- **A copper segment chains up to eight units.** The units' TX pair carries up to eight
  `DE`-gated drivers ordered by their slots; the master's pair, data RX at the unit, holds one
  driver that never turns. A node hears the master directly and its neighbours through its echo
  receiver. A glass port is point-to-point: the unit behind it shares the glass with nobody.
- **The parts, on `G-I-N-025`**: one `ISO1452` for the data in both directions, a second
  `ISO1452` as the **echo-check receiver** on the units' TX pair, its driver tied off and its
  UART's TX unconnected, and an `ISO1450` on channel B, its `D` and `R` both on the `CLK/PPS` pin.
  The `ISO145x` runs at a fixed baud up to 50 Mb/s with no rate-select pin and regenerates edges,
  so the 2²² clock passes the whole run. The plugged board says its medium on its `ID` resistor,
  and the firmware never guesses.
- **`DE` is populated everywhere, and it is the driver's power gate.** An enabled 485 driver
  burns ~48 mA into its termination whether it says anything or not; gated to the slot, the line
  side drops by the idle fraction — four fifths of it at a full segment's ~20 % occupancy,
  nearly all of it on a one-unit spur. Across sixteen spurs that is the difference between ~0,77 A
  of line-side drive held on and a small fraction of it. On the shared TX pair the UART's hardware
  `DE` (`DEAT`/`DEDT`, in bit times) turns the direction **inside the slot's guard bytes** —
  assert early, drop late, no firmware on the data path — and keeps the one-talker rule; the
  fail-safe termination holds the idle line while every driver sleeps. Full duplex removes the
  *turnaround*, not the gate: the master is never waiting on that pair. The clock drivers (~54 mA
  each) run continuously by definition.
- **`RE#` is not a pin.** The receiver is held active on the board, because every node hears its
  own transmission.
- **The echo check — every unit runs it, alone on its link or not.** The node reads back what it
  drove: DMA capture starts with the transmission, the echo's CRC is checked when it ends, and the
  frame is verified the instant it is out; a miss is a bus error, and the frame is held ready for
  the master's `RESEND`. The same receiver reads the neighbours' frames — their slot bytes against
  their timing check that the round runs in order, and a returning node takes its phase from them.
  The TX leaves on a channel the master's receiver does not sit on, so the check needs a **second
  receiver paralleled onto the TX channel** — the board's second `ISO1452` on copper, a
  receive-only tap into a second UART's RX on glass. On a point-to-point link the card's own CRC
  check covers the unit anyway, and running the echo there too keeps one code path
  (`../PROTOCOL.md` §3).
- **Termination: 10 Ω in series on both legs of every pair on every board, and 80,6 Ω across the
  pair behind them, put in by a jumper at both ends of a run** — on a chain, the board at the
  source end and the last unit's. The cable is UTP, so the target is ~100 Ω differential, and the
  two 10 Ω legs complete it: **the fitted part is 80,6 Ω, not 100 Ω.** The 10 Ω is a surge
  resistor first — it sits on every board, terminated or not, and halves the `SM712`'s current
  before the tube fires; that it completes the end board's ~100 Ω follows from it. 120 Ω belongs to
  dedicated 485 cable, which the station does not use.
- **Protection: an `SM712` at each pair**, behind the 10 Ω legs, on the Galvani board; the tube and
  the rest of the ladder are the source end's (`../../galvani/README.md`, *The protection ladder*).
- **In the box the clock to a unit is a buffered wire**, 33 Ω at the source; half a metre of
  board-to-board cable is not a transmission line and nothing regenerates it.

## How a bus starts and heals

**Bring-up has two modes and one order, and it may take minutes by design.** On RC, with the wire
clock already running and ignored, the structure is assembled: the RC dialogue, the numbers,
ranging, the tables, `BUSCFG`. Only then do the units take the clock, then `SYNC`, then the round —
floor by floor from the Bifrost down (`FLOOR` runs it per card; an Argus repeats the same ladder one
width down on its segments). Each floor starts on its own `SYNC`, fired on a second's boundary and
carrying that second's low byte, so floors started minutes apart count the same seconds, and
nothing races: Quake's 30-minute settling dwarfs all of it.

- **First sync — 38 400 on every NodBus.** Every NodBus — the spurs, the mini segments, the
  MasterNOD trunk — boots on RC at **38 400**, the one base rate: a fresh unit always knows it
  because there is only one, and the RC's ±1 % holds it with margin. After the procedure each link
  goes to its full rate: the trunk clockless, a spur onto the wire clock. **ModBus is not in this**:
  an arm is classic RTU at 9 600 or 19 200 from the first byte (`modbus.md`).
- **The spur clock runs from the moment the port is fed, and a unit does nothing with it until it
  is told to.** Nothing gates it for the start — the ranging pulse goes down the data channel, at
  start and in operation alike — and the one gate the clock has is the kill, `PORT_CLK`
  (`../PROTOCOL.md` §10). The sequence:
  1. `DISCOVER` broadcast → every unit answers with its tag → `ASSIGN_ADDR`, by tag, gives it its
     number and slot.
  2. **`BUSCFG` broadcast** — node count, frame-rate code, the rung → each node validates the clock
     it has been hearing — **N clean periods at 2²² counted against the HSI**, so a noise pulse on
     glass or copper cannot fake a clock — and switches HSE onto it, so its timer already runs on
     the wire; a mini unit takes the 2¹⁹ rung on a timer, not HSE.
  3. **`SYNC` broadcast is the start**, the last frame at 38 400, fired on a second's boundary: its
     header reads `unix.0` = that second's low byte and `frame` = 0, and each node **loads both at
     `SYNC`'s start-bit edge**, by timer capture with no CPU in the path, moves its UART to the rung
     and counts from there — `frame` to the rate and over, `unix.0` over every 256 s. A counter
     loaded while the timer still ran on RC would be ±1 % off the grid, which is why the clock
     comes first.
- **The index is the time's low end and nothing more.** A node never learns the date; the card
  holds the whole second from Kronos (PPS-K and the label) and prepends `unix.1..3`. The node's
  `unix.0` has to match the card's low byte for that frame — **a check, not arithmetic**; a
  mismatch is a slipped unit, and the frame goes up as filler with the reason. Sending the real low
  byte rather than zero is what makes the card's part a prepend: with zero it would hold an offset
  and add it to every frame. Two timers per node carry the whole time function — the free-running
  timebase with its capture channel, and one for outputs.
- **A unit with a persisted set** — number, slot and `BUSCFG`, persisted together under one check —
  does the same when it hears `DISCOVER`. Only in a running round, where `DISCOVER` never sounds,
  does it lock the clock on its own. **No `BUSCFG` is ever re-broadcast mid-run.**
- **The rejoin of one node — by itself, off the wire.** A reset node keeps its set, senses the live
  clock pair and re-locks by itself; no `SYNC` comes again. What it lost is the round's phase, and it
  reads that off the first frame it can decode: on a shared segment **a neighbour's DATA frame** —
  `unix.0 · frame` is the index, the slot byte and the width give the round's start — and on a
  point-to-point port **the card's `TICK`**, one a second in the gap after the last slot, whose
  header is the time, valid at its own start-bit edge. One frame, one capture, and it fires in the
  next round. The card sends nothing on demand; it learns of the rejoin because the slot fills. **A
  single node's reboot never costs the network a reset.** A node with no valid set — fresh, after
  `HARD_RESET`, a failed check — stays on 38 400 and silent until a sweep, and **a node whose sensor
  population changed does the same on purpose**, so the master and the archive re-learn what it
  carries.
- **`TICK` is a phase source, not a verification.** A running unit has no oscillator to drift — its
  time is the wire — and a unit that slipped anyway misses its slot, which the card sees the same
  frame from the position, the slot byte and the CRC. The slot structure checks in 7,8 ms what a
  broadcast would check in a second, so a running unit ignores `TICK`.
- **The mute rule:** a DATA frame exists only when the unit is synced. A node on RC never transmits;
  it buffers with the RC flag and listens.
- **The clock is the kill switch, and the tree makes it surgical.** Unnegotiated clock loss is a
  fault → RC, mute; the returning clock is the rejoin above. Every clock is generated by its parent,
  so the cut exists at every level: **one port's clock at its card** (`PORT_CLK` — a lone unit or
  one chained segment, nobody else notices) · **a card's four ports** (`PORT_CLK` 0 = all) ·
  **Kronos's drivers** (`GATE` 0 — the whole station). One unit on a shared segment is not the
  clock's job; that is the addressed `HARD_RESET`. The MasterNOD trunk carries no clock: a card
  whose trunk goes quiet buffers and resumes, and its spurs run on unaware.
- **The babbling driver is the one fault no protocol mitigates.** A driver stuck transmitting owns
  its segment. What exists: the `DE` gate is timed; a live node hears its own echo mismatch and
  releases; the master sees the CRC storm and probes with `HARD_RESET`. A hard-shorted driver is a
  sacrificial node — found by splitting the chain in halves, the box swapped. Accepted, in the same
  class as the direct strike.

## The rung — two, and the ×4 is the schedule

The units' TX pair has one talker at a time, so nothing is solved by allocating bandwidth; it is
solved by allocating *time*. The measurement stream is the only traffic with a fixed cadence, and a
full segment of eight at 128 frames/s needs

> **baud = 10 × units × 4 × 40 B × frames/s = 1,64 Mb/s**

Ten bits carry a byte (start, 8, stop), 40 B is the frame, and **×4 is a count, not a margin**:
every unit owns **four frame-times in every period** — its DATA frame, one complete reply or
resend, and two processor exchanges. Eight units buy a wire that carries about forty frames where
eight would do, and the thirty-two spare ones are named in advance. Rounded up to a power of two,
because the baud is an integer division of the timebase, that is **2²¹ = 2 097 152 baud**.

**Two rungs, chosen by the population.** The card counts the units that answered at enrollment and
hands the rung down in `BUSCFG`:

| units on the segment | rung | baud | 40 B frame | 16 B frame |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **one** — a point-to-point port, which is most of them | **2²⁰** | 1 048 576 | 381 µs | 153 µs |
| **two to eight** — a chained copper segment | **2²¹** | 2 097 152 | 191 µs | 76 µs |

**Nothing else is legal.** On a terminated line the reach is the clock channel's, not the baud's,
and 2²¹ carries the full eight. **The medium never decides the rung**: copper takes both; both glass
boards are point-to-point and so always 2²⁰, the 2 Mb/s module then in margin. The 10 Mb/s module
exists for the clock channel, never for the data. **The handset may pin either rung on a port**, for
a site that knows better than the count; it cannot name a third.

**Faster and then silent beats slower and busy.** `DE` gates the driver to the slot and a laser is
lit only while it says something. A lone unit at 2²⁰ occupies ~5 % of its period; the wire does the
same work at any rung, and the faster one shuts it up sooner.

**What the rungs carry.** At 128 frames/s both carry what stands on them by construction. At 256
frames/s a NodBus segment holds **five** units on 2²¹ and a mini segment eight; 4 × 256 and 2 × 512
fit. Eight NodBus units at 256 frames/s do not, and are not built. **A faster sensor keeps 128 frames
and puts two samples in the one 32 B payload** — no extra overhead, and the payload is a fixed block
precisely so this route exists. 128 frames/s is a *sensor* number, the rate the acquisition fronts
run at; the rung is its consequence.

**The segment stops at eight** because the card that forwards these streams and completes their time
does nothing else, and eight streams at 128 frames/s sit comfortably inside a part that is
deliberately underclocked, with software simple enough to read in one sitting.

## The slot schedule

**A slot is a frame plus guard bytes.** The dialogue lives in the ×4 between frames, never inside
one; the hardware `DE` turns inside the guard bytes; and the nodes cannot drift against each other
at all — one wire clock — so nothing pads for loose oscillators. A full segment of eight occupies
**~20 % of the line including guards**. A builder who wants a byte of idle between frames budgets
41 B instead of 40 — 2,5 %, never enough to move the rung.

```
ONE PERIOD AT MAXIMUM POPULATION — eight units at 128 frames/s, on the 2²¹ rung


  |◀──────────────── one period = 2¹⁶ ticks = 7,8125 ms ────────────────▶|

  ┌───────┬───────┬───────┬───────┬───────┬───────┬───────┬───────┬──────┐
  │  u1   │  u2   │  u3   │  u4   │  u5   │  u6   │  u7   │  u8   │ slack│
  └───────┴───────┴───────┴───────┴───────┴───────┴───────┴───────┴──────┘
   763 µs                                                    ~22 %, what
   each                                                      rounding the
                                                             computed baud
                                                             up to 2²¹ left


  ONE UNIT'S BLOCK IS FOUR FRAME-TIMES, AND ALL FOUR ARE ITS OWN

  ┌────────────┬────────────┬────────────┬────────────┐
  │    DATA    │  a reply   │   master   │    node    │
  │  its frame │ or resend  │   asking   │  answering │
  └────────────┴────────────┴────────────┴────────────┘
      191 µs       191 µs       191 µs       191 µs

  THE DATA FRAME IS ALWAYS FIRST AND ALWAYS THERE. The unit's own slow frames —
  its REPORT frame once a minute, a kind 5 answer — ride the fourth frame-time,
  behind the data; a reply owed goes before a report frame, which then waits a
  round (../PROTOCOL.md §5). A REPLY NEVER REPLACES A SAMPLE, and the unit's own
  three frame-times are where its ranging pulse goes.

  Each node fires at round start + (slot − 1) × width, computed once at init
  off the shared clock. The blocks are disjoint, so nothing arbitrates; a dead
  unit leaves its block empty and nobody shifts up. Only a node coming back
  reads a neighbour's slot byte, once, to find the round's start.
```

**The order: clock-anchored slots with a busy-hold and the echo check.** A unit fires at its own
clock-anchored deadline (`BUSCFG` announces the width), but arms the transmission only on a quiet
line — a predecessor still draining holds it until silence. After sending it compares its echo
against its transmit buffer. **A dead unit leaves a hole and nobody moves up**; at 20 % occupancy a
hole costs nothing. On a point-to-point port and at the master the busy-hold is vacuous, the channel
being theirs alone — one code path everywhere.

**The shape never changes; only the rung does.** Four frame-times per unit on a lone port at 2²⁰ and
on a full segment of eight at 2²¹ alike — one timing path, two values of one constant. **The card
decides the rung at enrollment, once**: in RC mode it walks the segment, learns how many units
answered, gives each its slot in `ASSIGN_ADDR`, and hands the rung and the frame-rate code down in
`BUSCFG`. From `SYNC` on the units are autonomous — they wait out their own offset and talk in their
own slot round after round with nobody polling them.

**Two frame lengths, and the reply picks.** CONTROL is a fixed **12 B** frame — the time, the
address, `SUB`, a verb and three arguments (`../PROTOCOL.md` §1). A node answers a query in CONTROL
where a byte or two says everything, or in a **full 40 B frame** where the answer is a payload. The
master asks in the gaps; nothing is scheduled for it, because the gaps are always there.

**Nothing is sent back to back.** A frame leaves one per slot. **`RESEND`** is one buffered frame
re-sent in the unit's own slot, by its index.

## Time in the frame

**A frame starts on a timer instant, not a software event.** 2²³ ticks a second over 128 frames is
**2¹⁶ ticks a frame, exactly**, so a frame's number places it whether or not anyone was listening
when it went out.

**The index is two bytes**: `unix.0`, **the second modulo 256** — wrapping every 4 min 16 s — and
`frame`, **the sample within that second**. Each node counts both off its own timer, nothing is
broadcast to keep them running, and the absolute second is added at the card. A frame therefore says
which second it belongs to even when it is sent late, which is what lets a buffered frame be placed.

**The sub-second position divides twice, both times by a power of two:**

| layer | divisions | bits | step |
|---|---|---|---|
| frames in the second | 128 | **7** — a byte | 7,8125 ms |
| ticks in the frame | 65 536 | **16** — two bytes | ~119 ns |
| **full position** | **2²³** | **23** | one tick |
| what is delivered | 2²⁰ | 20 | ~954 ns |

The three bits between 2²³ and 2²⁰ are what a **two-byte record field reaching across eight frames**
costs — two bytes over 8 × 7,8125 ms is 2⁻²⁰ a step. They are alignment, not a reserve of precision.
The two layers are 7 bits and 16, so a two-byte field carries one of them, never both.

**The delivered step is the project tick, 2⁻²⁰ s ≈ 0,954 µs, and sub-second time is binary
station-wide.** A frame is 2¹³ = 8 192 project ticks; an event time is the frame index shifted
(× 8192) less a backward offset in the same ticks, with a borrow into the second — shifts and one
subtraction, exact on the binary clock, no conversion constant anywhere on the station. **Decimal
microseconds exist only at the exporters**: an HMC record carries the second and the frame index natively, and
every exporter multiplies by the one constant 10⁶/2²⁰. **The record's own
quantisation, ±0,48 µs, is the largest single term of the budget** (`gps-pps.md`, *The precision contract*),
and it is accepted: a return stroke's channel is kilometres long, and its onset reaches two stations
smeared by about a microsecond whatever the record does. **A correction computed finer than a tick
is kept finer and rounded once, at the end** — Tesla's `LAT` and the CFD's sub-sample fraction are
in quarter-ticks (2⁻²² s) and only the field is whole ticks.

## The one second

**Every record reaches the head exactly one second after it was measured — 128 frames, on every
path.** A stamp is the measurement instant by construction — the index counts from the moment the
sample was taken, and the card completes it — so the delay never touches a stamp. A late frame is
not a wrong frame. **The second is built out of circular buffers, each a two-gate FIFO**: a frame is
written when it arrives and read at a fixed instant, its own index plus the buffer's delay; a frame
not there at its instant leaves as a filler. The delays add to 128 on every path:

| path | the unit | Argus | Bifrost | total |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| a unit on a Bifrost port | 32 | — | 96 | **128** |
| a sonde behind an Argus | 16 | 16 | 96 | **128** |

**A unit holds `0xFF13 DELAY` frames**, written by its parent at floor-up and persisted
(`../PROTOCOL.md` §7), **never fewer than 8** — a floor fixed in the image, because the unit's buffer
is what `RESEND` is answered from; Tesla's backward offset, 8 frames at most, sits inside its 32.
**A Bifrost writes 32 to every unit on its ports and holds 96**; an Argus told 32 holds 16 and writes
16 to its sondes. Memory is nowhere the limit: 8 × 96 × 48 B ≈ 37 kB of the card's 272 KB, 32 × 40 B
≈ 1,3 kB on a unit. **The head aligns nothing** — its frames arrive one second after the sample and
it writes them; a second is complete at the head one second after it ends.

## The delay tables — every one, and who owns it

Corrections come from tables, never from fields in a frame. **The tables are built and held in the
units at run time; the firmware hard-codes only the dictionary** — frame formats and per-TYPE
payload layouts. Every measured table is readable over the control plane and lands in the head's
configuration mirror, where the handset shows them in one place. Two numbers are typed at a site —
Kronos's `CAB` and `INT` — and everything else is measured or fixed by design.

| table | built by | held by | refreshed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Kronos's delay table — `CAB` the antenna cable, `INT` the antenna and receiver as one number, `ROUTE` a remoted receiver's run | the head types `CAB` and `INT`; Kronos ranges `ROUTE` | Kronos — subtracted from the captured source edge before PPS-K is placed | on write; `ROUTE` on `RANGE` |
| a port's ranged delay | the card's `FLOOR` (ranging) | the unit — written to it as `ROUTE` and applied once at `SYNC`, the grid placed that much early; nothing downstream subtracts (`../PROTOCOL.md` §7) | at floor-up; then every `RANGE_INTERVAL` seconds per port, automatically — a change over 2 ticks is written and reported |
| a mini segment's delay | the Argus's `FLOOR`, the same pulse | the sonde, the same way; the Argus applies its own route on its up port | at floor-up; then every `RANGE_INTERVAL` per segment |
| the unit's own `SKEW` — its capture synchroniser and its communication board's channel difference | the datasheets: a house table by `ID`, zeros where both channels are one part family | the unit, `0xFF12`, applied with `ROUTE` | never — a constant of the image |
| the ranging turnaround | fixed by design — the unit's own timer, capture and compare | the card | never — a constant |
| the time bus's fixed delay, Kronos → cards — a `DS91C176` and a `THVD1450` in series, 3,4 + 25 ns typical from the datasheets, plus ≤ 2 ns of ribbon: **four ticks of 2²⁷** | the datasheets | the card — its second is placed four ticks before the captured PPS-K edge | never — a constant |
| the wiring — who is on which port and segment | the sweep | the card and the master's roster; reported up as the segment table (`../PROTOCOL.md` §10) | on any population change |
| the ModBus rosters | provisioning, verified at bring-up (`../PROTOCOL.md` §7) | Palatine | on provisioning |
| the one second — each unit's `DELAY` (32, a sonde 16) and the card's own (96 on a Bifrost, 16 on an Argus) | the design, written by the parent at floor-up | the unit's `0xFF13`, the card's `0xFF40 + port` | at floor-up |
| Pip's path delay per transmitter | Pip, from the station's position and the table in its image | Pip — subtracted from each carrier's decoded second | on `POSITION` |
| a sensor's own conversion schedule onto the grid (Gauss, Pascal) | the unit's interpolation, by construction | the unit | never |

## The oscillators

Which board carries which oscillator is `clocks.md`'s: one precise part at Kronos and commodity
everywhere else — the cards and the NodBus units off the wire, the mini units on the HSI
disciplined by the rung, the ModBus MODs on the house crystal. This document keeps what NodBus does
with the clock it is given.
