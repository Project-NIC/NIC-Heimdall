# Gaia — the graveyard

Rejected alternatives and superseded states, with the reason. Nothing returns from here and
nothing is deleted from it.

## The 10-sonde ring around every island — superseded by the sector fan

Every coastal array site took a full ring of ten sondes. **The tsunami source map is closed** — the
known subduction arcs — so the threat bearing is computable, and the lee shore sees diffraction,
not a wave front. A 4–5-sonde fan on the source-facing sector replaced the ring: ~800 sondes do
what ~1 900 did. The ring stays only on an island within reach of two arcs.

## Lobos de Afuera as Peru's islet — failed the slope test

Chosen by the islet-first rule, dropped when measured: **shelf-locked, 210 km to the slope toe**.
An islet must sit on the slope, not the shelf.

## Pip as the time source of a station with no sky view — superseded

Siting rule 6 read "where the station has no sky view and takes its time from Pip instead of GNSS",
the antenna "outside or at a window". **The GNSS receiver lives in the enclosure, and a site whose
mast cannot see the sky is sited wrong**; Pip stands outside as the SID channel and a second time
source.
