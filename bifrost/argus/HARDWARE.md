# Argus — hardware

> **Design-stage concept.** The figures are verified against the parts' sheets and the parts
> before a board is made.

**What differs from a Bifrost, and Argus at a distance.** The card itself — its pins, sockets,
supply and parts — is `../HARDWARE.md`.

## What Argus is, electrically

**Argus is the Bifrost card.** Same STM32H523, same **six USARTs of the H523's seven — one
upstream, its echo receiver on the sixth, four segments** — same connectors, the same three bucks. What separates the two is
the level on the time bus's `ATTN` pin — a card pressed on Kronos's ribbon is a Bifrost, a card
off it an Argus, one firmware
([`../HARDWARE.md`](../HARDWARE.md)). **Argus has no pin table of its own: the
card's socket tables in `../HARDWARE.md` (*The sockets — pin by pin*) are Argus's, the one
difference being the rung its down ports carry, 2¹⁹.** **No LPUART on a bus**: a mini segment is
TDMA and needs a continuous ear.

**Four segments is not a port count, it is where three limits meet** — the timer captures the
ranging needs, the USARTs, and the frame index. Argus tiles its segments **four 8 B mini
payloads to a 32 B payload** and takes **⌈minis/4⌉ NUMBERs and slots upstream** (`README.md`):
four full-rate Gauss take one slot, eight take two — each NUMBER's index run stays under the
256/s per-address ceiling.

It carries **no line drivers, no isolation and no protection ladder** — each port's Galvani boards
do that (`../../galvani/README.md`). There is no line transceiver on
Argus; the two `THVD1450` time-bus receivers are fitted and hear nothing.

| per | what leaves the board |
|---|---|
| mini segment (×4) | a **12-pin data body and an 8-pin power body**; the power board taps the 12 V wire on its own terminals |
| board | the NodBus upstream — the fifth USART, its echo receiver on the sixth — on its own data body and power body |
| board | three I²C controllers for the five power bodies' `INA238`s — I3C1, I2C3, I3C2, at most two sockets to one |

**`CLK` is populated on every port, upstream and segments alike.** Mini is NodBus: it carries the
wire clock, and that is the whole reason a unit goes on a segment instead of on a ModBus arm. The
segments' clock is Argus's to drive, regenerated from the clock it receives upstream — every
bus's clock is generated by its parent (`../../core/blocks/nodbus.md`).

**The rung is the CARD's, not the segment's, and on an Argus it is 2¹⁹ = 524,288 kHz — all four
segments, always.** Upstream Argus takes the 40 B bus's 2²²; downstream it hands every segment the
one number, out of `MCO1` at prescaler 8 through one `SN74LVC126A` quad buffer (`../HARDWARE.md`). **The
mini-NOD takes the sync on a timer capture rather than on HSE** and divides it to its own grid by
whole powers of two, so a lower rung costs the unit no accuracy: the error is the edge, and the
edge is ns-class and fixed (`../../core/blocks/nodbus.md`).

**Why 2¹⁹ and not 2²²: no mini-NOD wants more, and one medium cannot carry more.** `Quark-Tubes`' bins
are already written on it — `÷4096` of 2¹⁹ is the 128 Hz frame (`../../quark/README.md`) — Gauss
free-runs its POLLs and interpolates onto the grid in software, and Pascal divides the same way.
Meanwhile 2¹⁹ is 1,05 Mb/s NRZ equivalent against a square wave's double, which is what puts it
inside the **2 Mb/s** module a long optical segment carries, where 2²² needs a 10 Mb/s one. The
low number therefore serves every segment a build can put here, and the card needs no per-segment
decision. Nothing about the 40 B bus moves.

**A segment's `ENABLE` and `LINE_EN` are one GPIO, its clock gate's `OE` a second, and its `SD` a third**, in the card's pin
budget (`../HARDWARE.md`); none is time-critical. The upstream port is a unit end: its
`LINE_EN` is 10 kΩ to 3,3 V in the socket and its `ENABLE` is not connected.

## One segment per UART, and no fan-out at all

```
        MCU                         12-pin DATA body
        TXD ───────────────────────  TXD ─┐
        RXD ◀──────────────────────  RXD  ├─ boards ── one mini segment
        DE ────────────────────────  DE   │
        MCO1 ── SN74LVC126A gate ─ 33 Ω ─  CLK ─┘
                  OE_n

        × 4 — one MCO1 output into all four gates, an OE each — and the fifth USART goes
        upstream on its own data body and power body
```

**There is no gate, no AND and no merged receive.** A TDMA segment has to be heard continuously,
so it gets a UART to itself.

**`DE` is per port, and it is per segment because a port is a segment.** It stays what the
contract makes it everywhere — the driver's power gate, enabled around the slot and dropped
between, so the ~48 mA a driver burns into its termination stops flowing for the idle fraction.
Where the segment is copper and chained it also keeps the one-talker rule.

**The receiver is held active on the board — `RE#` is not a pin.** On a segment Argus is the
master, and a master never echo-checks: `RXD_ECHO` is not connected on a down port. The echo
check is the upstream port's, where Argus is a unit — `RXD_ECHO` on the sixth USART.

## Supplies — 12 V in, three bucks

**The card taps the 12 V wire on its own two terminals — the battery rail beside the head, the
unit power board's 12 V at a remote Argus — carries none of it across the board, and makes 3,3 V on
three bucks**: a `TPS629206` for the processor, a second for the **up** port, and an `LMR43620`
for the **four down** ports. **A short behind any socket then costs that branch and not the H523.**
None of the three has its `EN` on a pin — every rail runs whenever the 12 V is there — and a
sleeping card keeps the up port listening while each down port is dark by its own
`ENABLE`/`LINE_EN` and its clock gate's `OE` ([`../HARDWARE.md`](../HARDWARE.md), *The
card's supply*). **The two terminals are Degson `DGPS2.5R-5.0`**, two-pole, 5 mm, 0,75–2,5 mm² — the
input and the tap, the same node.

**The card draws 3,2 W at 12 V** in the all-optical worst case — 2,92 W on 3,3 V through the buck
— and well under a watt where its ports are copper. A communication board has no terminal and no
buck; the 3,3 V and the logic cross the connector together, and a source power board on a port
taps the 12 V wire on its own terminals.

**Telemetry is the Bifrost's, because it is the same board.** Each of the five sockets — the up
port and the four segments — carries its power board's `INA238` behind that board's `ISO1642`,
and **the socket sets the address**: `A_SEL` is strapped low on the first socket of a controller's
pair and high on the second, so a bus carries 0x40 and 0x41 and **two sockets is the ceiling on
one controller**. The card therefore spreads the five over I3C1 (the up port and segment 1), I2C3
(segments 2 and 3) and I3C2 (segment 4), and every socket has its own `ALERT` pin, high on alarm
([`../HARDWARE.md`](../HARDWARE.md), *Peripherals*; `../../galvani/README.md`). None
of it is Argus's to decide — an Argus is a Bifrost that read `ATTN` low — but a segment's power
board is plugged by whoever builds the site, so the strap is the site's to get right.

**That is what makes the port kill one wire.** `ENABLE` on the power body kills the port's converter, and `LINE_EN` on the data body switches the
line side of a copper communication board — the `SN6505B`; nothing switches an optical module. **One GPIO per port drives both**, joined on the board, as on the Bifrost.

## Argus at a distance — it is a site, not a spur unit

**A remote Argus is a site, and its size is set by its optics.** Count the modules, not the boards.
**A communication board carries two 1×9 seats** — the data channel full duplex and the clock channel
one-way (`../../galvani/HARDWARE.md`, *The fibre front*) — and the one-way channel populates `VccT` only
at the sending end and `VccR` only at the receiving end. So Argus's own draw is **four segment ends
sending the clock plus one upstream end receiving it**, and nothing else: **glass carries no power, so
every far unit is its own site with its own feed.**

**Every optical part this project fits reads `I_TX + I_RX` = 100 mA** — the 10 km `OPT10` part and
the 2 Mb/s 100 km part alike. The sheets give it as one figure; the working split is
**~75 mA the laser / ~25 mA the receiver**, an estimate that matters only on the one-way channel. The
data laser is dark between slots, so an end's average sits well under its ceiling, and the card's
rails are sized on the ceiling: both sections of the data module and the one section of the clock module.

| | per end, average · ceiling | on 3,3 V at the ceiling |
|---|---|---|
| **a segment end** — data module + clock TX | ~105 · 175 mA | 578 mW |
| **the upstream end** — data module + clock RX | ~55 · 125 mA | 413 mW |
| **the card** — H523 and its two `THVD1450` time-bus receivers, on its own 3,3 V | 61 mA | ~203 mW |
| **five ends and the card** | **~536 · 886 mA** | **2,92 W** |

**How a remote Argus is fed — the cell, the 300 V pair where the modules run hungrier, the 12 V wire on
site — is the Galvani boards' business** (`../../galvani/README.md`, *A remote Argus*).
